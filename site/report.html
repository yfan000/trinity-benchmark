What the numbers survived

These figures are what remained after repeated rounds of removing defects in the measurement itself. No model changed while reported scores moved by tens of rows:

  • A rule that could not be satisfied. content_markers_present applied a file-detection heuristic as a completeness requirement, demanding the literal amino-acid alphabet inside every FASTA.
  • A rule contradicting another rule. not_copied_from_example penalised reproducing the HPLinpack header that header_and_output_lines requires fatally. Retiring it in r28 is worth 10 rows (188/320 → 198/320), all of them in Input preparation.
  • Prompts demanding the impossible. Three anchors instructed models to author compiled binaries (.tpr, .re2, .h5) that fatal rules forbid writing.
  • A prompt naming a queue that does not exist. All four models were told queue workq for HPL on Crux, which has no such queue; all four obeyed and were failed fatally. Eight failures attributable entirely to the harness.
  • A silently truncated worked example. The prompt builder cut examples at 44 lines, so GROMACS models saw one of three required file formats.
Limits of this result Ten anchors per subtask makes a single cell ±1–2 rows; 20 of 320 cells flipped between judge replicates. The judge’s own test–retest spread is 7% of rows at k=3, so differences under about 5 points are not resolvable. One severity judgement is worth more than that spread: scoring no_directive_comments as minor rather than major would read 206/320 instead of 198/320, almost all of it gpt-oss (audit.matrix --demote prices it). Every number is GPT-judged only; the same-family question against gpt-oss-120b is unmeasured. Input-preparation failures were verified by reading the evidence individually; the other subtasks were not audited to the same depth. The grades were collected under r27 and re-scored offline under r28, which is exact for a rubric change that only removes rules.
